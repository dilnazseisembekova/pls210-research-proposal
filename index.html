PLS 210 · Research Proposal

Curricular Exposure & Epistemic Authority

How is exposure to Euro-American scholarship related to Political Science students’ views of Kazakhstan-based scholarship at Nazarbayev University?
Population · NU Political Science students IV · Exposure to Euro-American scholarship DV · Views of Kazakhstan-based scholarship
Why this question?

Worth asking — and researchable.

Curricula shape which scholars and knowledge traditions students encounter most frequently. Existing research identifies inequalities in global knowledge production and a strong position for Western knowledge in Central Asian higher education. What remains less clear is whether differences in curricular exposure relate to how students evaluate locally produced scholarship.

Empirical

Both exposure and students’ views can be observed and measured rather than answered normatively.

Scoped

Political Science students · Nazarbayev University · 2026–2027 academic year.

Answerable

Assigned readings can measure exposure; survey responses can measure students’ views and allow comparison.

Epistemological positioning

Why positivism fits

My question looks for a systematic relationship between two concepts that can be operationalized and measured across students.

Independent Variable Exposure to Euro-American scholarship
→
Dependent Variable Views of Kazakhstan-based scholarship

Comparing students with different levels of exposure allows me to examine whether variation in the IV is associated with variation in the DV. Because the proposed design is observational, an association would not by itself establish causation.

Contribution

From a broad hierarchy to a student-level question.

01

What we know

Research identifies inequalities in global knowledge production, debates over Eurocentrism in university teaching, and a prominent position for Western knowledge within Central Asian and Kazakhstani higher education.

02

What is less clear

Whether differences in students’ curricular exposure to Euro-American scholarship are associated with how they evaluate Kazakhstan-based scholarship.

03

What I add

I examine this relationship among Political Science students at Nazarbayev University, focusing on variation in curricular exposure and student perceptions.

Literature Review

From global to local

Global knowledge production

Alatas (2003) · Academic Dependency

Social-science knowledge production is globally unequal: academic communities outside dominant Western centers can depend on those centers for ideas, research agendas, methods, and standards of academic excellence.

Tenzin & Lee (2022): Academic dependency remains relevant two decades later, although changing Asian academic systems and decolonization efforts complicate the original framework.

Political Science teaching

Deo & Caivano (2026) · Decolonial Pedagogy

Political Science teaching can reproduce Eurocentric assumptions when dominant perspectives structure the curriculum; simply adding diverse authors is not sufficient to decolonize how the discipline is taught.

University curriculum

Seckinelgin (2023) · Curriculum & Epistemic Injustice

In the studied university context, students challenged a curriculum centered on the UK, Western Europe, and the US, while established disciplinary knowledge continued to occupy the core and other perspectives could remain positioned as alternatives.

Central Asia

Hwami et al. (2024) · Internationalization in Central Asia

The systematic review identifies Westernization, Englishization, modernization, and the reproduction of unequal relations as recurring patterns in research on higher-education internationalization across Central Asia.

Kazakhstan + students

Hwami (2024) · Geopolitics of Knowledge

Internationalization in Kazakhstan promotes Western knowledge; the English-speaking West is treated as a center of knowledge and learning, and Western educational credentials are highly valued among the participants studied.

The research gap

What is still unanswered?

The literature moves from global academic hierarchy → university teaching → curriculum → Central Asia → Western knowledge in Kazakhstan.

Does variation in curricular exposure to Euro-American scholarship relate to how NU Political Science students evaluate Kazakhstan-based scholarship?

This is the specific relationship my study addresses.

Theory and hypotheses

Proposed mechanism

Academic dependency provides the theoretical starting point for the expected relationship.

Global academic hierarchy
→
Euro-American scholarship as a dominant academic standard
→
Greater curricular exposure
→
Greater perceived authority of Euro-American scholarship
→
Lower relative authority of Kazakhstan-based scholarship
H1. Greater curricular exposure to Euro-American scholarship is associated with lower perceived academic authority of Kazakhstan-based scholarship among Political Science students at Nazarbayev University.
